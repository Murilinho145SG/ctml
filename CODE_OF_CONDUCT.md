# Code of Conduct

The CTML project aims to provide a respectful, welcoming space for everyone who participates in good faith. We want discussions to stay focused on building and improving native interfaces and the tools around them.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with respect, including when you disagree.
- Give clear, constructive feedback about ideas and code rather than attacking people.
- Respect stated boundaries and take responsibility for the effect of your words and actions.
- Credit sources and contributors accurately.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, personal attacks, or repeated unwanted contact.
- Threats, encouragement of harm, or sexualized behavior that is unwelcome or out of place.
- Sharing someone's private information without permission.
- Impersonation, retaliation against someone who raises a concern, or deliberate disruption of project spaces.
- Spam or promotion unrelated to the project.

## Scope

This code applies to project spaces, including issues, pull requests, code reviews, and other channels used for CTML work. It also applies when someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a concern privately using any private contact method listed on the [project maintainer's GitHub profile](https://github.com/Murilinho145SG). Include links, dates, and enough context to understand what happened. Please do not post sensitive reports in public issues. Reports will be handled as confidentially as practical, and retaliation for raising a concern in good faith is not acceptable.

If no private contact method is available, or if the concern involves the maintainer, use GitHub's platform reporting tools.

The maintainer will review reports and may take action based on the impact and context. Actions can include a request to stop, a warning, temporary restrictions, or removal from project spaces. Serious incidents may skip earlier steps. People involved will be given a reasonable opportunity to provide context, while prioritizing safety and privacy.
